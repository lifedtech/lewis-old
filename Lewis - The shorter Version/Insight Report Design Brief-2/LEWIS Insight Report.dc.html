Line thickness follows how strongly the link is evidenced. The arrow marks the one link that has a direction.

Your sleep and your emotional load Strongest, directional

Your sleep and your emotional load are showing up together. In research, short or broken sleep reliably makes it harder to regulate mood the next day. You also told us your movement has been minimal, and across randomised trials regular activity has a consistent effect on low mood and distress.

That does not mean sleep and movement are the whole story here, but they are the thread worth pulling first.

Evidence: established

09 The one thing

You told us you want one thing to focus on, not a plan. So this is one thing.

Cue Action

Tonight, after you put your phone on charge, step outside for five minutes before you sit back down.

Why this one

Your body is where one change reaches furthest. The barrier you named there was time, so this is five minutes. You told us you have tried before and it did not stick, so this is smaller than what you probably tried last time, and it is attached to something you already do every night rather than to an intention to remember.

What the evidence says, and where it does not

Regular physical activity has a consistent, well replicated effect on low mood and distress across randomised trials. Doing it outdoors has additional support, though the studies that found it typically used around ten minutes rather than five. We are starting you below the researched dose on purpose, because a five minute thing you actually do beats a ten minute thing you do not. If it holds for a week, ten is the next step.

Why it is shaped this way

The specific form, “after X, I will do Y”, is not a stylistic choice. Attaching a new action to an existing cue has meta analytic support in its own right, independent of what the action is.

What this report is not asking of you

Nothing about money this month. Your money reading is one of the hardest in this report and you named money itself as the barrier, which no next step can move in a week. It is in the report because it is true, not because it is this month’s work.

What this is not

LEWIS is a reflection tool. It does not diagnose anything, it makes no claim about hormones, brain states or heart rhythms, and it is not a substitute for talking to a doctor or a therapist. The research behind it describes what tends to be true across many people. It cannot tell you what is true for you, only what is worth looking at.
